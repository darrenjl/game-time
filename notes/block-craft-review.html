Block Craft: making the quests visible

The quest system was firing correctly all along — it was written in English at the top of the screen, where neither boy could read it. This is what was wrong, what the evidence was, and what the game does now.

The diagnosis in one line: the quest system was fine and firing correctly — it was just written in English at the top of the screen. Oisín is 6 and Finley is 3. After the first quest there was no arrow, no highlight, no spoken prompt: only a white bar of words. Meanwhile the first quest's target, the trunk, sat buried inside a canopy four times its size that gave nothing when tapped.

Now: every quest points at something and says itself out loud, leaves give wood, one tap does the whole job, and the panels stop the world while they are open.

This was verified by serving the real game and driving it in a browser, plus a headless harness built from the game's own physics and terrain functions. No console errors, on either the Start or the Continue path.

Answering the question directly: are the quests showing?

Yes. The banner renders on both entry paths and the chain advances correctly. Driving the real page:

Start -> banner visible, "Tap a tree to get WOOD! 🌳" / "Wood: 0 / 3" Continue -> banner visible, same text (loaded save, quest 0) console errors: 0 canopy tap @250,200 Wood: 0 / 3 -> Wood: 0 / 3 canopy tap @320,150 Wood: 0 / 3 -> Wood: 0 / 3 canopy tap @255,225 Wood: 0 / 3 -> Wood: 0 / 3 canopy tap @190,225 Wood: 0 / 3 -> Wood: 0 / 3 canopy tap @320,225 Wood: 0 / 3 -> Wood: 0 / 3 trunk tap @447,290 Wood: 0 / 3 -> Wood: 1 / 3 trunk tap @447,350 Wood: 1 / 3 -> Wood: 2 / 3 trunk tap @447,410 Wood: 2 / 3 -> "Tap ⚒️ and make PLANKS!"

Five taps on the obvious green target produced nothing. Three taps on the brown blocks completed the quest instantly. The system is not broken — the target is just invisible, and the reward for tapping the wrong thing is indistinguishable from the reward for tapping the right one.

The critical findings

Critical Only quest 1 has a pointer; quests 2–8 are text only

drawQuestPointer opens with if (creative() || questIdx !== 0) return;. The bouncing gold arrow — the one piece of non-verbal guidance in the game, and its own comment says it is there so that "a child who cannot read the banner" knows what to do — exists for the first quest only. From quest 2 onward the child gets a sentence and nothing else.

It is worse than neutral at that point. On quest 2 the banner reads "Tap ⚒️ and make PLANKS!", but the only highlighted button in the toolbar is Mine, because the gold border marks the selected tool. The single visual emphasis on screen is pointing at the wrong control.

Recommended fix Two changes, both small. First, drop the questIdx !== 0 guard and give every quest a target: a world cell for the mining quests, and a pulsing ring on the toolbar or swatch button for the crafting and building ones. Second, speak the quest. speakStart() already exists and the boys already have the 🗣️ button — call the same utterance path with q.say whenever the quest changes.

Critical Four out of five taps on a tree give nothing

The first quest is "Tap a tree to get WOOD! 🌳". A tree is 3 trunk blocks and 13 leaf blocks. Leaves have drop: null — mining one plays the same chop sound, throws the same particle burst, and gives nothing. The wood counter stays on 0 / 3.

The canopy is also the visually dominant part, and it is drawn in almost the same green as the ground, so it reads as scenery rather than as the thing being asked for. Told to tap a tree, a child taps the big green blob, gets full audio-visual confirmation, and makes no progress.

trees on the map: 16 | avg spacing: 10.0 columns LOG cells (give wood): 48 LEAF cells (give nothing): 186 chance a tap on tree pixels yields wood: 20.5%

The quest arrow does aim at the top trunk block, but it floats half a block above it — which puts the arrow graphic itself inside the leaf row. A child who taps the arrow hits a leaf and gets nothing.

Recommended fix Give leaves a drop. TYPES[T.LEAF].drop = 'wood' is a one-word change and makes the whole tree do what the quest says it does. If you'd rather keep the Minecraft distinction, at minimum play sNope() instead of sChop() for a zero-drop block, so the child hears the difference.

Critical The top of every tree can never be reached

Reach is B * 4.6 measured from the hero's mid-body, which sits 0.775 blocks above the ground. The upper two rows of leaves sit 4.72 and 5.72 blocks up. They are outside reach even standing directly underneath. Tapping the crown of a tree walks the hero to its base and then stops, permanently, no matter how many times it's tapped.

total tree cells: 16 cells unreachable even standing directly beneath them: 6 LEAF at dc=-1 height=5 -> distance 4.72 > reach 4.6 LEAF at dc= 0 height=5 -> distance 4.72 > reach 4.6 LEAF at dc= 1 height=5 -> distance 4.72 > reach 4.6 LEAF at dc=-1 height=6 -> distance 5.72 > reach 4.6 LEAF at dc= 0 height=6 -> distance 5.72 > reach 4.6 LEAF at dc= 1 height=6 -> distance 5.72 > reach 4.6
Recommended fix Raise heroReach() from B * 4.6 to B * 6.2. That covers the whole tree and most of the visible screen, and there is no downside for a young child — long reach is forgiving, not cheaty.

Critical An out-of-reach tap throws away what you asked for

In onTap, a tap outside reach calls walkTo(wx) and returns. The hero walks over — and then nothing happens. The cell you tapped, and the tool you had selected, are never remembered. Every distant object needs two taps: one to travel, one to act, with a pause between them where the game appears to have ignored you.

Combined with the reach radius, this is most of the screen:

visible play area: 12 x 8.5 blocks reach radius: 4.6 blocks share of the visible world you can act on: 62.5% share where a tap only walks you (no action): 37.5%
Recommended fix Queue the intent. Store pendingCell = {c, r, tool} alongside hero.target; when the walk finishes in updateHero, if the cell is now in reach, run the action. This is the highest-value change in the whole list — it converts every "the game ignored me" moment into a small piece of automation that looks like the hero understood.

The frustrations underneath

High 🔥 Fire is free, permanent, and sits next to Build

igniteCell costs nothing and needs nothing. Fire spreads on its own tick, consumes logs, planks, leaves and wool, and there is no undo. The toolbar order is Mine · Build · Fire · Craft · Photo — Fire is one 58px button away from the tool the game just told them to use to build a house, and quest 5 is literally "build a house".

Recommended fix Take Fire out of the survival toolbar. It's a sandbox toy and Building Blocks already exists for that. If it stays, require a torch in the bag to light anything, so it costs something a child has to earn.

High Twelve of the fourteen block swatches are empty

SURVIVAL_ITEMS renders all 14 swatches at all times. At the "place 10 blocks" quest a child owns dirt and stone. The other twelve are dimmed to 35% but remain tappable, and tapping one selects it, then every attempt to place gives a rejection buzz. That's a row of buttons that look like buttons and behave like walls.

Recommended fix In buildSwatches, render only items where have(item) > 0, and rebuild the row on give/take. The palette then grows as the child mines, which is its own small reward.

High The last quest gates on 85 seconds of waiting

Quest 8 ("Night is coming — bonk a monster!") needs a monster. Monsters spawn only when darkness() > 0.55. With DAY_LEN = 200 and dayT starting at 0.05, that's 85 seconds of real time after the world starts. The banner announces the goal and then the game does nothing for a minute and a half.

dusk begins at t = 74 s monsters can spawn (darkness>0.55) at t = 85 s
Recommended fix When the child reaches the final quest in Fight mode, push dayT forward to dusk. The night arrives because they earned it, which also teaches what the ☀️/🌆/🌙 badge means.

Medium There is nothing to do after the quests end

The chain finishes with "🎉 You did it all! Now build whatever you like." and the banner hides after five seconds. Diamonds are generated below row −9 and a Diamond weapon recipe exists, but no quest ever points at them, so in practice they're never found. The most exciting thing in the game is invisible.

Recommended fix Add a ninth quest — "Dig deep and find a DIAMOND 💎" — and a tenth for crafting the diamond weapon. It reuses systems that are already built and gives the game a real ending.

Things that turned out to be fine

What the game does now

  1. Every quest points at something. The questIdx !== 0 guard is gone. Mining quests put a bouncing arrow on the nearest block of the type they want, or a cyan dig-here arrow at the hero's feet when it is still buried; crafting and building quests pulse the toolbar button they need.
  2. Every quest says itself out loud when it changes, through the same speech path as the 🗣️ button.
  3. Leaves give wood, and the canopy greens moved away from the ground green so a tree reads as a separate object rather than scenery.
  4. One tap does the whole job. A tap out of reach is remembered; the hero walks and acts the moment the target comes within arm's length, then stops. Firing on reach rather than on arrival matters — the hero often never arrives, and the old behaviour dropped the intent silently.
  5. Reach is B * 6.2, up from 4.6, so the whole tree and most of the screen are in play.
  6. 🔥 Fire is gone from the survival game, along with its spread-and-burn machinery. Building Blocks is where burning things belongs.
  7. The palette is what is in the bag. Swatches appear as items are mined instead of showing fourteen buttons that only buzz.
  8. Night arrives with the quest that asks for it, not 85 seconds later.
  9. Two diamond quests close the chain — find two diamonds, craft the diamond weapon — so the deep part of the world has a reason to exist. Diamonds were made slightly less rare to suit.
  10. The world stops while a panel is open. Monsters were still walking up and taking hearts while the craft and how-to-play panels were covering the screen.
  11. Quests can be skipped. The pause menu carries ⏭️ Skip quests, which flips to 🎯 Do the quests again.
  12. The start panel fits a short screen. New World shares the Start row, the pickers shrink at 700px and 560px of height, and touches inside an overlay now scroll it — the global scroll lock had made anything below the fold unreachable.
